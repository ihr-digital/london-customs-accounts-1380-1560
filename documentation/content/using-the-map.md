# The Map

The Map shows the **corpus gazetteer**: every place the accounts name that can
be placed — 235 of them — drawn from the editors' own indices.

## What is drawn

- **Places named in the accounts** — one disc per place, **sized by how often
  the accounts name it**. Calais is named 1,970 times; most places once or twice.
- **Regions named in the accounts** — sixteen rows are areas rather than ports
  and are drawn as outlines. Where possible the outline is **dated to the
  period** (the Kingdom of Portugal 1415–1801, not modern Portugal).
- **London** — a crown, always shown. Every voyage in the corpus touches it.
- **Customs ports, 1566** — a later administrative geography, **off by default**.
- **Inland navigation** — navigable waterways (Oksanen, 2019).
- **Water, c.1500** — the coastline and inland water of the period (Viabundus).

## Using it

- **Click a place** for its popup: how often the accounts name it, the span of
  the volumes that do, the spellings they use (Calais appears as *Calesiam*,
  *Calisia*, *Caleys*, *Calecium*), and its external identifier.
- **Labels** — the busiest ports are labelled at every zoom; the rest appear as
  you zoom in, to keep the view readable.
- **The key**, bottom right, switches each layer on or off.
- The view is **bounded to the gazetteer**: you cannot pan away from the places
  the accounts mention.

## Filter mode

- The key's last switch, **Follow the table filters**, is **off** by default.
- Switched on, the map shows only the places evidenced by the ladings currently
  passing the Table's filters, and says how many that is.
- It works by the spelling in each lading's heading — the account writes
  *Brekilsey*, and the gazetteer knows that is Brightlingsea.
- Switch it off to see the whole gazetteer again.

:::{note}
A place appears in filter mode only if a **lading heading** names it. A place
mentioned only inside a cargo description will not put a marker on the map.
:::
